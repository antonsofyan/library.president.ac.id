Cikarang, August 27 — The Adam Kurniawan Library at President University hosted a Turnitin Workshop for Instructors specifically designed for faculty members and doctors at President University’s Faculty of Medicine. Led directly by a Reference Librarian, this workshop was designed to optimize the use of the academic integrity detection platform in support of an academic environment grounded in integrity.

The main focus of this training was the Quick Submit feature, which allows instructors to quickly check drafts of scientific articles, research publications, and medical assignments without having to create a virtual classroom first. The Reference Librarian demonstrated the process from uploading documents to retrieving analysis results, enabling faculty members to efficiently conduct independent evaluations of academic manuscripts that require prompt attention.

In addition to Quick Submit, participants received technical guidance on the “Creating Class” feature for managing student assignments. Through this feature, faculty members in the Faculty of Medicine can create structured assignment submission spaces, set deadline limits, and configure manuscript scanning parameters. This system makes it easier for medical instructors to monitor the history of student academic work submissions in a single, organized dashboard.

The session continued with a key discussion on interpreting Similarity Reports and AI Reports. The Reference Librarian analyzed the parameters used to calculate text similarity percentages, methods for excluding citations or standard medical references, and indicators for detecting content generated by artificial intelligence (AI-generated text). A deep understanding of these two reports is essential for faculty members to provide objective and proportionate evaluations.

The participants’ enthusiasm was evident in the lively discussions and interactive questions posed by the doctors throughout the event. A variety of technical questions arose, ranging from the similarity index tolerance limits for medical journals to the accuracy of the AI Report in distinguishing technical medical terms from generative text. Through this workshop, faculty members at President University’s School of Medicine are expected to be able to apply standards of academic integrity more rigorously and with greater confidence (Laurensius Yoel Fabian).
